Access settings

LIAM mode: two large answer buttons, one step at a time, a still map, and a separate Next button. Every mapping task stays available. Use ChromeOS Face Control or your usual pointer to click; this activity does not use the camera.

Hold-to-select is optional. Leave it off if your face-control tool already generates clicks. There is no timer or penalty for trying again.

About the maps

Historical polygons use André Ourednik’s Historical Basemaps GeoJSON for circa 1400. Borders are approximate reconstructions at continental scale. Ireland identifies the island; the England outline includes Wales as on the classroom map. Castile remains separate from Aragon and Granada. The dataset’s mainland “Sicily” is labelled Kingdom of Naples, with island Sicily shown separately. Genoa uses a supplementary approximate mainland coastal outline. Overseas possessions of the Italian republics are omitted. The OpenStreetMap background shows modern place names and borders; coloured outlines show the historical teaching layer.

The separate Ottoman GeoJSON overlay is digitised and approximately georeferenced from the assignment’s 1683 reference. Its coastline and frontier alignment is less precise than the main Europe layer. Its ruling religion was Islam, but the empire contained many religious communities.

Yellow, red and green follow the assignment’s teaching colour key. The Renaissance spread over centuries through many routes; the arrows show classroom connections rather than dated journeys. Spain is grouped with the northern destinations as instructed in the assignment.

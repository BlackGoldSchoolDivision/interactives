About this illustrated atlas

The complete classroom assignment includes 37 mapping steps and six written questions. LIAM mode uses two large buttons, a still map and separate Next controls. It works with ChromeOS Face Control or your usual pointer; the activity does not use a camera.

Explore shows every map name. Names remain hidden throughout the mapping questions. The Italian steps automatically show a closer view.

Europe’s coastlines and rivers use Natural Earth 1:50m data. Circa-1400 historical boundaries use André Ourednik’s reconstruction, with the classroom adaptations used in the original Renaissance activity. The separate Ottoman map follows the assignment’s approximate 1683 reference over Natural Earth coastlines. These are different historical periods.

Yellow Italian states, red northern destinations, green England, city stars, crosses and arrows follow the assignment’s teaching key. Routes represent classroom connections across centuries. Ireland identifies the island; Castile is one of several Iberian kingdoms. Italian overseas possessions are omitted. Historical borders are approximate.

The paper, blue wash, ship and engraved mountains are decorative artwork. Mountains show broad areas, rather than exact elevation.

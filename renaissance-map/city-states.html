How this activity works

Start with the map. Investigate Florence, Venice and Genoa. Then rank the three cities using a clear criterion and evidence.

City, city-state, island: a city-state was a city and the territory it governed. Rome and Naples are cities; Sicily is an island. Italy was divided into many states during the Renaissance.

LIAM mode: two large answer choices, shorter instructions, sentence choices and optional hold-to-choose. Press Listen to hear the current task or city information.

Your ranking is a judgement. Different rankings can work when the reasons match your criterion. Think about sea access, connections, wealth and challenges. Geography helped create opportunities; people, trade agreements, skills and money mattered too.

Map: exact coastlines and river positions come from Natural Earth. Paper and mountain illustrations come from the existing Renaissance Atlas. This is a geographic activity, not a map of political boundaries. Trade lines show simplified connections, not exact voyages. The Arno route should not be read as a sea route or as proof that large ships reached Florence.

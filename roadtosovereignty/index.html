1914: Who Controlled Canada’s Foreign Affairs?

✓ Completed

When the First World War began in August 1914, Canada was a self-governing Dominion—but Britain still controlled Canada’s foreign affairs. That meant Canada did not make its own separate decision to declare war. Toggle the timeline switch below to see what happened!

Fighting as Distinct Canadian Units

Before 1914, Canadians who fought overseas were often folded into British units. During WWI, Canada formed distinct military forces. Tap both cards below to flip them and inspect their significance.

Canadian Expeditionary Force (CEF)

Formed in 1914 • Overseas Service

What made the CEF historic? →
CEF Dossier Tap to flip back ↻

Over 600,000 Canadians enlisted in the CEF to serve overseas. Britain sent these Canadian forces to Europe where they served as distinct Canadian units for the first time rather than simply blending into British regiments.

Sovereignty Link: Built national pride and separate Canadian leadership.
Tap to Flip ↻ 🛡️

The Canadian Corps

Four Unified Divisions • Western Front

How did it shape Canada's reputation? →
Canadian Corps Dossier Tap to flip back ↻

Formed in 1915, the Canadian Corps united Canada’s four divisions into one powerful fighting force—eventually led by a Canadian commander, Sir Arthur Currie. Their teamwork earned global respect.

Sovereignty Link: Proved Canada could organize, plan, and lead independently.

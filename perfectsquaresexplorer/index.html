Perfect vs. Non-Perfect Squares

Use the slider to change the Area of the square. Notice how the side length changes. A perfect square has an area that results in a rational side length (like an integer). A non-perfect square results in an irrational side length (a decimal that goes on forever without repeating).

Mathematical Breakdown

Side Length is: Rational Number

Because 1 × 1 = 1. The side length is an integer.

Every positive real number has exactly two square roots. For Area = 1, they are 1 and -1. (Side lengths are always positive).

Classify Numbers

Sort the numbers into their correct sets. Remember: Rational numbers include integers and natural numbers. They can be written as fractions (a/b where b≠0) and are terminating or repeating decimals.

Numbers to Sort

Rational Numbers (Q)

Terminating/repeating decimals, fractions.

Integers (Z)

Whole numbers and their negatives.

Natural (N)

Counting numbers (1, 2, 3...)

Irrational Numbers

Non-terminating, non-repeating decimals (e.g. √non-perfect square).

Did you know?

  • Zero (0) is the only real number that has exactly one square root (√0 = 0).
  • A rational number can always be written as a fraction a/b, where a and b are integers, and b ≠ 0.
  • Fractions like 1/3 result in repeating decimals (0.333...) which are written with a bar: 0.̅3. These are rational!

Sources & map data

The historical shapes come from Natural Resources Canada’s Atlas of Canada, under the Open Government Licence – Canada. The explanations adapt the Atlas timeline for classroom use.

  • Original territorial evolution interactive
  • Official dataset and file geodatabase
  • Canadian Heritage: historical boundaries
  • Manitoba: Métis negotiations and Manitoba’s entry
  • Nunavut Legislative Assembly: how Nunavut was created

Use the GeoJSON

Each of the 23 dates has a separate GeoJSON file in WGS 84. Boundaries are simplified for display; these are historical Atlas snapshots, rather than a present-day legal boundary survey. The final snapshot is 2003.

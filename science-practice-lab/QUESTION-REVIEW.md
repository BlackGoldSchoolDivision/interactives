# Matter question and visual review

Reviewed 20 October 2026. Scope: all 54 original Matter practice questions, including stems, answer keys, distractors, hints, explanations, diagram evidence, accessibility descriptions, and response controls.

## Changes that matter most

- Rebuilt liquid particle models with irregular arrangements, equal particle counts and sizes, and varied motion directions. Removed misleading translational arrows from solid models.
- Replaced text-only phase arrows with actual forward/reverse arrows. Removed destination descriptions and image captions that supplied answers.
- Corrected the thermometer scale pointer, added a below-zero reading, and made temperature reference conditions explicit.
- Corrected the ice explanation: cooling produces surface ice; lower density keeps formed ice afloat. Improved the lake and equal-mass volume diagrams.
- Replaced repeated numerical tasks with evaporation evidence and an equal-mass volume table. Added measured bridge movement from both sides of a gap.
- Improved distractors and selectable source sentences. Read aloud includes diagram descriptions and table data.
- Preserve unrelated saved progress and responses. Reset only four substantially revised tasks; older completed round reviews containing those tasks are retired so they cannot show obsolete answers under new questions.

## Evidence and limits

The bank still contains 54 questions: 18 for each Matter KUSP group, two in each of nine formats per group, mapped to all 29 supplied curriculum statements. These are original practice items. This review does not establish equivalence to the official PAT or replace hands-on assessment.

The volume and balance tables are example observations for practice. Scientific models exaggerate small spacing or length differences. Phase models represent a typical substance; water's unusual freezing expansion is treated separately.

Scientific checks used primary sources:

- [American Chemical Society: Molecules in Motion](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter1/lesson2.html) — liquid particle motion and spacing with heating/cooling.
- [American Chemical Society: Moving Molecules in a Solid](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter1/lesson4.html) — vibration about fixed positions and thermal expansion.
- [USGS: Water Density](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-density) — ice's lower density, floating, liquid versus solid arrangement, and near-freezing behaviour.
- [NIST: Thermometer Verification](https://www.nist.gov/pml/sensor-science/thermodynamic-metrology/mercury-thermometer-alternatives/mercury-thermometer-7) — reference points, placement, and dependence of boiling temperature on pressure/elevation.

Validation is recorded in `validation/browser-report.json`. The browser suite solves every answer key, exercises six viewport sizes, checks resize preservation and start-menu navigation, tests revised saved-question handling, and captures every desktop question plus mobile visual questions. Individual captures and contact sheets are in `validation/question-review/`.

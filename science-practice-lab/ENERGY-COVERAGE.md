## Source and conventions

Curriculum: [Science 1–6 Curriculum NEW Alberta](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1N34FqElKkGYrWeKEeLhOaJ1JZjz6yKfL9tKO1-Fmdqk/edit), Grade 6 column A, rows 43–103. Content was read through the native Google Sheets connection. Knowledge, Understanding and Skills & Procedures statements are visible in Teacher view. The stray suffix on “biofuel” in the source was cleaned in the app's displayed text; the source sheet was not changed.

- Internal and external forces always use a named object or system boundary.
- Elastic and plastic changes are classified using observations after release, within the tested conditions and elastic limits.
- Action–reaction pairs occur simultaneously, with equal strengths, opposite directions and different receiving objects. Ordering questions ask for an analysis or comparison process, not a time sequence between the paired forces.
- “Before processing” and “after processing” follow the curriculum's resource-use examples. Direct use means without first making electricity or a manufactured fuel; direct use still involves energy transfer and conversion.
- Planning credits, site records, test measurements and availability-day records are labelled examples. Questions do not claim current Alberta generation shares, real project costs or dated policy requirements.
- Alberta contexts include natural gas for heating and refined petroleum fuels for transport. Resource choices compare availability and access, societal, economic and environmental effects.
- Respect for land and resource rights is taught as a management responsibility without inventing a legal approval procedure.

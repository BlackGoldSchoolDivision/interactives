ON THE INTERACTIVE BOARD

Teacher notes

Big question: What happens to the shape of a material when we push, pull, or bend it—and what happens when we let go?

Suggested routine: Show one object. Invite a prediction by pointing to one of two pictures. Test it. Ask students to describe what changed and whether it returned to its original shape. Repeat with real classroom materials.

Four small experiments

  • Sponge: Squeeze a dry sponge gently. It usually springs back.
  • Modelling clay: Squish soft clay. It holds its new shape.
  • Rubber band: Stretch gently. It usually returns to its original shape when released.
  • Pipe cleaner: Bend a chenille stem. It generally stays bent.

Questions to ask

  • What did you predict? What did you observe?
  • Which materials went back to their old shape? Which stayed changed?
  • Is it still the same material after its shape changes?
  • Can you find something else that behaves the same way?

Curriculum connection: Grade 1 Science—investigating observable material properties, physical changes, and making predictions based on evidence. This is one focused investigation, not complete outcome coverage.

Safety and accuracy: Use gentle force. Keep elastic bands away from faces; avoid overstretching. Real materials vary in thickness, temperature, wear and force. This visual model is illustrative, not a precise physical simulation.

Accessibility: All student actions are click/tap/keyboard accessible. The speaker reads questions aloud using the device's speech engine when available. Students can respond by pointing instead of reading.

Sources & map data

This expanded edition uses two published Natural Resources Canada Atlas of Canada resources, under the Open Government Licence – Canada.

  • Before 1600: geographic context with modern coastlines, without provincial borders or invented Indigenous boundary polygons.
  • 1667–1791: cropped map panels from the fourth edition’s Territorial Evolution, 1667–1873 sheet. Printed claims, dates, colours and disputed areas are preserved. Numbered place markers are classroom annotations.
  • 1840 / 1841: the Act of Union passes in 1840 and takes effect in 1841. The map is explicitly a 1849 reference map, showing the united Province of Canada alongside later changes. It is not an exact 1841 snapshot.
  • 1867–2003: the original 23 Atlas boundary snapshots, rendered as selectable political regions.

Colonial colours represent European claims and administrative boundaries. They do not mean that Indigenous nations disappeared, consented to these claims, or surrendered all rights. Historic source terminology is retained in the printed maps and explained in the place notes.

Teacher guide

One journey: pack at Fort William, paddle, portage, travel inland and trade at Rainy Lake. The Posts view offers separate North West Company and Hudson’s Bay Company trading practice.

Start with play

  1. Pack food, a repair kit and goods for trading. Secure the load.
  2. Paddle to the landing. Carry the canoe and every bundle across the portage, then reload.
  3. Choose an inland crossing and bargain for four food days and four pelts within the canoe’s capacity.

Use the on-screen controls and Hint when needed. Returning to camp gives one button to resume the saved journey. Start a new journey resets that journey; the company practice visits remain separate.

Keep the history visible

The setting is 1809. Fort William and Fort Lac La Pluie belong to the North West Company network; York Factory represents the Hudson’s Bay Company network. Indigenous routes and trading networks existed before these companies. Anishinaabe knowledge, canoe technology, guiding and provisions were essential around Fort William; Cree and Assiniboine partners were important to HBC trade.

The art is illustrative. Weights, bargains, crossing costs and travel time are invented game rules. Map connections are simplified, and company lines do not show ownership of Indigenous lands. The game cannot represent every community or experience.

After playing, ask: What did carrying more cost? Whose knowledge made this route possible? What would we need to research about women, Métis communities, unequal power and environmental change?

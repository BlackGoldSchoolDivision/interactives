# Trading posts and objects — researched visual checkpoint

The game’s company-rivalry setting is 1809. These are source-informed illustrations and fictional practice visits, not measured reconstructions or documented 1809 transactions. Switching posts does not represent travelling between them.

| Post | Evidence used in the scene | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fort William · North West Company | Kaministiquia canoe rendezvous; depot, warehouses, canoe work and trades; Anishinaabe dwellings described in 1805 and 1808 images. | Building placement, foreground porch, weather, workers and shelves are imagined. The park’s current living-history presentation is 1815 and is not proof of an exact 1809 layout. |
| York Factory · Hudson’s Bay Company | Hayes River lowlands; Old Octagon built 1788–1795, with four pentagonal flankers linked by roofed curtain sheds. | The illustration suggests the earlier form. The surviving white depot began in 1831 and is deliberately excluded. The 1810 Northern Department headquarters role is not assigned to 1809. |

Goods evidence:

- York Factory: Karklins and Adams identify a sealed Octagon deposit from 1795 to before 1815 in which white, bright blue and red glass beads predominated (2013, printed pages 92–93). The game’s bead colours are inspired by this archaeological group. The image is not a replica of an accessioned bead or a traditional beadwork design.
- NWC network: McGill’s exhibition describes wool textiles, metal kettles nested for canoe transport, iron axes shipped in crates and knives. These are network-level object types, not excavated sets from Fort William. Similar goods passed through HBC networks; neither company has an exclusive kettle or cloth style.
- Fort William: official historical background identifies smoked fish and wild rice as provisions produced by Anishinaabe people. Wild rice is offered only at Fort William in this checkpoint. Parks Canada documents Indigenous trading networks and Cree fishing/hunting at York Factory.

Both companies’ operations depended on Indigenous expertise, technologies, provisioning, labour and relationships. A bargaining activity covers only part of this history; later lessons must address unequal power, environmental changes and consequences for Indigenous communities. Daily needs, bundle quantities, weights, barter thresholds and the food/fur goal are invented game rules, not historical prices or general Indigenous preferences.

## Gameplay boundary

The two post visits are separately saved trading practices with visible shelves, offers, counter-offers and inventory changes. Learners can compare different supplies and demands without moving their canoe to a distant post. A completed, current portage can supply a separate practice copy of the carried cargo, including remaining repair supplies and food. The journey’s original inventory stays available for the next river leg. Connecting trading to an actual inland arrival is a later chunk.

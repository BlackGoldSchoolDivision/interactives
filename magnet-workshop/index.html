Magnet Workshop · Teacher notes

Grade 4 Science, Alberta: Non-contact forces of gravity and magnetism. Here students predict and observe attraction, repulsion, pole direction, changes in effect at different distances, and attraction to selected materials.

Suggested routine: Let a student predict, test without changing the setup, describe the observation, and revise their thinking. Repeat with real classroom magnets and small steel objects.

Three investigations

  • Poles: Like poles repel; opposite poles attract. Use the Flip button to test a new pole orientation.
  • Distance: Magnetic force generally becomes weaker farther from a magnet. This uses an illustrative strength scale, not a calibrated force measurement.
  • Magnetic or Not: Paper clips, steel bolts and steel washers can be attracted. Wood, plastic and aluminum generally are not attracted to ordinary classroom magnets. Objects made with different alloys may behave differently.

Discuss with students

  • Which pole combinations push? Which pull?
  • Does a magnet need to touch an object to exert a force?
  • When did your prediction match? What did you change after observing?
  • How could you test magnet strength fairly with a real magnet?

Model note: Cart displacement is illustrative and visually exaggerated. Force depends on magnet shape and strength, materials, orientation, and distance. Avoid interpreting this animation as a calibrated physics simulation.

Safety: Supervise use of small magnets and metal objects. Keep magnets away from small children who could swallow them, and away from sensitive equipment or medical devices.

Inspired by the McCord game

This short digital adaptation keeps the twelve item scores, three starting dice rolls, two trading rounds and zero points for unexchanged starting stock. The museum cards call fur values “gold pieces” and goods values “prestige points”; this version uses “trade points” for both.

The PDFs supplied by Darren include the rules and item cards, but omit the separate dice lookup cards. This version reconstructs a six-face lookup from the examples and paired value tiers. Computer partners, their changing priorities, visible shelves and untimed rounds are new game rules; the classroom team rotation and uneven-team auction are not included.

The setting is a fictional Great Lakes trading post in New France around 1750. Illustrations and characters are imagined. Anishinaabe trading parties are shown as experienced partners; preferences belong to individual fictional parties. The numerical scores are game mechanics, not documented historical prices or a description of any culture’s values.

Discuss: Why might both sides want the same bargain? What happened when you waited? What does this small game leave out about relationships, Indigenous knowledge, colonial power and the environment?
